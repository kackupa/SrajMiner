# Round-planet world design

## Goal

Each destination should read and play as one connected round planet. The miner can travel all the way around its surface, drill radially inward through the core, and emerge on the opposite surface. The live scene now has charted polar terrain, seam travel, wheel zoom, and an orbital cutaway view. This document tracks the remaining integration and playtest work.

The [orbital cutaway concept](prototypes/orbital-cutaway-concept.png) illustrates the intended transition from the familiar wrap-map to the complete globe. It is an original visual reference for discussion, not game art or a screenshot of the current build.

## Current geometry

New Cryo Shelf expeditions use 126 columns and a 40-row (480 m) radius; other unvisited destinations use 471 columns and a 150-row (1,800 m) radius. Existing saves retain their saved geometry, including the previous 201-column, 64-row (768 m) starter and the legacy 942-column, 300-row (3,600 m) chart. Four guaranteed ship signals scale across the smaller starter while preserving their order through the core. Both use the same twisted terrain seam. `MiningScene` projects terrain as polar cells and smoothly rotates the camera frame to keep local outward at screen-up around the planet. Zoom has two distinct stages: the local scene zooms out to a clear 0.62× handoff, then a short animated transition replaces it with the separately fitted orbital globe view. This avoids lingering in the distorted middle zoom range; zooming back reverses the transition before local zoom resumes. Reduced-motion settings skip the handoff animation. The surface seam stays camera-continuous; crossing the gravity center creates the deliberate animated half-turn. The pod can move across the seam with radial motion mirrored. Hab 07 buildings and platforms now use curved tangent/radial coordinates on either surface. Charted-world collision now tests the miner's screen-oriented hull against the rendered polar cell polygons and stops at the last clear position instead of resolving curved cells against rectangular tile bounds. Radial cuts expand their tangent swath as the arc length narrows, and a 72 px physical center chamber gives the pod clearance while preserving the drillable core relic. A disposable Chromium check verified the previous compact-world core crossing at 1,742 m with full hull, 139 L fuel, and no overlaps; the new shorter opening still needs a fresh browser run and human pacing check.

The Cryo starter scales both dimensions together: at 12 m per tile, `R = 40` and `W ≈ π × 40 = 126` keeps the globe round and shortens the core trip. Other newly visited worlds use `R = 150`, `W = 471`; older charted saves retain `R = 64`, `W = 201` or the legacy `R = 300`, `W = 942`.

The Cryo starter chart has a diameter of 80 rows and a surface loop of about 252 chart tiles (3.0 km). Other compact charts have a diameter of 300 rows and a surface loop of about 942 chart tiles (11.3 km). Previous starter saves retain a 128-row diameter and 402-tile surface loop; legacy charted saves keep a 600-row diameter and 1,885-tile surface loop. Chunk streaming supports all seeded sizes. Ore pickup reach and salvage-magnet range/velocity use physical polar distances on charted planets, including at the narrowing core; magnet line of sight samples the projected world path so it cannot pull through solid terrain. Charted collision uses the same polar projection: candidate cells are culled from the miner's rotated physical hull, then checked as curved polygons. Radial drilling cuts enough adjacent angular cells for the hull to pass through the narrowing bore; the highlighted cut and beam preview reflect that width. The core chamber clears cells by physical radius, and the map's core relic remains the center obstacle until mined. Legacy rectangular maps retain their prior collision and pickup rules.
