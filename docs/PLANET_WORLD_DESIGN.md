# Round-planet world design

## Goal

Each destination should read and play as one connected round planet. The miner can travel all the way around its surface, drill radially inward through the core, and emerge on the opposite surface. The live scene now has charted polar terrain, seam travel, wheel zoom, and an orbital cutaway view. This document tracks the remaining integration and playtest work.

The [orbital cutaway concept](prototypes/orbital-cutaway-concept.png) illustrates the intended transition from the familiar wrap-map to the complete globe. It is an original visual reference for discussion, not game art or a screenshot of the current build.

## Current geometry

New expeditions use 471 columns and a 150-row (1,800 m) radius, while migrated campaigns retain the legacy 942-column, 300-row (3,600 m) chart and their saved coordinates. Both use the same twisted terrain seam. `MiningScene` projects terrain as polar cells, smoothly rotates the camera frame to keep local outward at screen-up around the planet, and blends the full gameplay view to an orbital cutaway at extreme zoom-out. The surface seam stays camera-continuous; crossing the gravity center creates the deliberate animated half-turn. The pod can move across the seam with radial motion mirrored. Hab 07 buildings and platforms now use curved tangent/radial coordinates on either surface. New campaigns now use the compact 1,800 m radius; old campaigns intentionally retain their 3,600 m progression. Charted-world collision now tests the miner's screen-oriented hull against the rendered polar cell polygons. Radial cuts expand their tangent swath as the arc length narrows, and a 72 px physical center chamber gives the pod clearance while preserving the drillable core relic. This keeps old rectangular Mars campaigns on their original collision model and does not change the save schema. A disposable Chromium check verified a clean compact-world core crossing at 1,742 m with full hull, 139 L fuel, and no overlaps. Full fresh-save drilling/pacing and whether the automatically widened cuts feel fair still need human playtesting. Underground structure art and lighting also remain open.

The compact world scales both dimensions together: at 12 m per tile, `R = 150` and `W ≈ π × 150 = 471` keeps the globe round and halves the core trip. Older charted saves retain `R = 300`, `W = 942`.

The compact chart has a diameter of 300 rows and a surface circumference of about 942 tiles (11.3 km). Legacy charted saves keep a 600-row diameter and 1,885-tile surface loop. Chunk streaming supports both seeded sizes. Ore pickup reach and salvage-magnet range/velocity use physical polar distances on charted planets, including at the narrowing core; magnet line of sight samples the projected world path so it cannot pull through solid terrain. Charted collision uses the same polar projection: candidate cells are culled from the miner's rotated physical hull, then checked as curved polygons. Radial drilling cuts enough adjacent angular cells for the hull to pass through the narrowing bore; the highlighted cut and beam preview reflect that width. The core chamber clears cells by physical radius, and the map's core relic remains the center obstacle until mined. Legacy rectangular maps retain their prior collision and pickup rules.
