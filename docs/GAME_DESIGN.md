# Game design — Cold Signal

## Campaign promise

Begin on the Cryo Shelf beside your landed mothership. Drive the miner out, drill for ore and the planet’s unique core, then return to the ship and press E to board. After the first core activates the jump drive, W takes the mothership into orbit and S lands it back on its pad. Core fuel powers only travel between planets; miner fuel still powers local flight and drilling.

## Expedition loop

Launch from the mothership’s surface pad, descend by drilling, and return through persistent tunnels. Deposits have deterministic yields of 0.5, 1, 2, or 3 units; their crystal density communicates value before cutting. Cargo has a strict shared capacity, and multi-tile cutters warn before a cut would exceed it. Sell ore, service the pod, and invest credits in upgrades. Mine a planet’s unique core to record its archive entry and add four interplanetary jumps; each jump costs one core-fuel unit. Local miner fuel remains reserved for thrust and drilling.

The Cryo Shelf has optional luminous route-data seams in its central shaft. Mining one records its signal and pays a one-time $200 survey grant; it is no longer a ship part or travel requirement. The mothership’s drive is ready to launch, while unique cores supply its route fuel: the first core also activates interplanetary flight, and each core found on another world adds four jumps. Every jump costs one core-fuel unit. Preserve a separate local-fuel meter for pod flight and drilling. Five planetary core records chart Vesper-9. Optional navigation hashes add fictional offline checksum collectibles and short lore entries without paying credits or creating any exchangeable value. The archive derives a survey ledger from each region’s saved depth and collectible records. Ore sales remain available for upgrades, services, and optional tools. Every region has an independent seed and persistent excavation record. Each new visit begins at its own surface hub.

## Miner growth and risk

Seven upgrade tracks improve drill speed, fuel capacity, cargo capacity, hull integrity, engine thrust, scanner range, and automatic grapple reach. Tracks continue beyond level 5 with rising prices and diminishing stat gains; milestone gates unlock later level bands, while scanner levels after full horizontal coverage reveal deeper vertical bands. Drill levels 3–5 cut two, three, then four tiles in a centered swath, with later tiers gradually widening the cutter. Wide cuts use proportionally more drill fuel; the pod's compact collision body remains unchanged for shaft travel. The optional grapple must be purchased before it catches a dangerous fall; it needs clear, higher rock within range, briefly hangs the pod so the player can thrust away, and never catches through solid rock. Upgrades extend its reach and reduce cooldown. Fuel exhaustion or hull failure recovers the pod and forfeits unsold ore, while banked credits, upgrades, discoveries, and tunnels remain.

Only fuel, hull, and cargo need managing. Gravity itself uses no fuel. Long uncontrolled falls cause velocity-based hull damage; short drops are safe. Emergency recovery prevents a stranded or bankrupt save from becoming unplayable.

## World and presentation

Hab 07 develops into a vertical mining town as planetary cores are recovered and colonies expand. New one-way decks can be landed on and dropped through with Down, mirrored by local gravity on the far hemisphere. Elevator frames, work cabins, transit, archive antenna, and beacon make permanent campaign milestones visible at the surface. Keep town growth tied to those milestones rather than adding recipe crafting or another resource ledger.

Charted worlds use a round, polar tile layout sized to each saved planet; terrain streams in deterministic 16×16 chunks. Legacy uncharted Mars saves retain the 48-tile-wide layout they were created with. Seeded yields, ore distributions, cave frequency, palette, strata names, and per-region factors create different descents. Prism Fault adds rare luminous geodes: each is clearly outlined, called out by the drill target, and guarantees three units of ore. Mars Frontier has warm, orange thermal seams and Hull Graveyard has mint-lit ark-alloy caches; both are uncommon, give at least two units, and are highlighted in the target readout. All finds derive from map seed and tile coordinate, so they survive chunk reloads and old save formats need no change. The explored map shows dug tunnels only and never reveals hidden ore. Return-fuel telemetry estimates vertical ascent plus reserve; it cannot predict detours or piloting.

The art is original procedural pixel-style terrain and equipment. Mining charges purchased at Hab 07 open a small diamond-shaped blast after a readable 1.2-second fuse. They eject ore as physical pickups instead of placing it directly in cargo; cargo space and piloting determine how much the player recovers. The optional salvage magnet pulls loose pickups toward the pod within 190 pixels when no solid block obstructs the path; it still respects cargo capacity. Pickups, fractional remainder, and armed fuse state persist with the region. The scanner begins with a tight four-tile field, upgrades to cover a planet's circumference, then reveals deeper vertical bands. A purchasable stasis module holds vertical position while X is held, consuming fuel. The first Rock Swimmer arrives after 420 m, phases through intact geology, and homes toward the miner; its glow and proximity cue warn of an 8-hull collision. The drill can repel enemies with clear, repeated hits, while up to three automatic sentry turrets intercept cave threats within 440 px. Later encounters add the Shard Manta's telegraphed charge, with a Hull Scrapper replacing it on Hull Graveyard. All seven upgrade tracks contribute to miner artwork growth up to a 1.84× scale, while its collision body stays compact for excavated tunnels. An original quantized synth score gains rhythmic layers during movement and drilling; recovering route data or an archive hash adds a short, distinct four-note hook on the next beat. Quick mute and separate music/effects levels persist locally. The mothership lands at the surface hub and waits for the miner to return with a planetary core. Its long-range drive uses core fuel, separate from local miner fuel; press E to board and W/S to take off or land. Pilot specializations offer faster deep-rock cutting, wider scanner reveal, or a larger ore hold; they are free to switch while docked, and the UI prevents leaving cargo above a smaller hold's limit. Visual ore cues keep resource value legible without audio. The Hab style bay sells pod finishes, pilot suit palettes, hull decals, and silhouette profiles; profiles pair a rendered frame shape with a cabin-glow color without changing collision or handling.

## Save and scope

The four optional regional navigation hashes unlock named crew voice logs in the archive. Recovering all four reveals the final message and closes the initial mothership signal mystery; hash recovery reuses the existing milestone save list, so old saves need no migration. Each planet has a sealed core sample and an evidence record. The first five unique records chart Vesper-9; recovering its Return Bloom completes the six-world ledger and reveals the crew's home port. Core records reuse their existing milestone IDs, so this story reveal needs no save migration.

Version 26 local saves keep campaign progression separate from each map's seed, location, excavation, exploration, ore pickups, armed charge, buildings, and warehouse. Boarding state and core fuel are durable too. Navigation hashes and the six planetary core records reuse the existing milestone list. Grapple cooldown and tether are momentary physics state and do not change save data. Version 1–25 saves migrate while retaining their campaign maps and discoveries; old Mars campaigns keep their original geometry. Older versions receive defaults for fields added over time, and the version-20 migration preserves a grapple only when it had been upgraded beyond its former free tier. Players can export JSON saves and import a validated save after reviewing its campaign summary. There is no backend, wallet, token, or blockchain integration. Navigation hashes are fictional, offline collectibles with no monetary value.

This is an expanding playable campaign foundation, not yet a finished long-form RPG. Six worlds and the first crew-archive arc are implemented; sustained human balance and feel tests remain. Further chapters and a richer restored-ship hub are later possibilities. See [the roadmap](NEXT_SPRINT.md) and [playtest findings](QA.md).

## Planet-core campaign direction

The current campaign takes the miner through the cores of six distinct planets. Each destination has its own sky, gravity/rock palette, strata, signature resources, hazards, and core chamber. Every destination has a sealed core record in the crossing path. The core halo provides bounded 360-degree steering and temporary extra drill reach so the route can be explored around the center; recovering a record pays a one-time $3,000 claim on the first four worlds, $4,500 on Cinder Vale, and $6,000 on Vesper-9. The first five records chart Vesper-9; its Return Bloom completes the ledger and reveals the crew's home port.

### Endgame: cross the core

The core is a midpoint that opens a second half of the same planet. After crossing the protected chamber, the far-side gravity points back toward the core; the 180-degree view reorientation makes this feel like an inverted hemisphere, and W thrusts outward toward its crust. Returning through the center flips the view and gravity back. The mine now has both surfaces, local depth and return-fuel readings, a one-time durable crossing reward, and a service dock at the far crust. A HUD cue explains the pull direction and outward thrust.

This uses 360-degree mouse drilling while steering or thrusting; upgraded swaths follow the aim vector and protect tiles overlapped by the pod. The flipped camera transform also reverses pointer aim and streamed tile rendering. Gravity-aware grapple, impact handling, physical drops, charges, hazards, local return estimate, docking, service, and save-derived orientation are implemented. System tests cover a far-crust return and mirrored aim/save state. A prepared-bore browser route verifies both crossings and save/reload on either hemisphere; ordinary mining through the passage and human feedback on transition timing and readability remain. No crossing should strand a save or duplicate excavation.

### Endless-feeling upgrade curve

Upgrade tracks continue beyond level 5 with rising prices and diminishing stat gains. The credit-based purchase path remains available at the workshop; milestone gates open levels 6, 11, and 16 after core records, and progression has no hard level cap. Upgrade values, prices, pod scale, save validation, and workshop display support levels beyond 5. Old saves retain their current levels.

Upgrades answer concrete planetary challenges: grapple stability, drill width/hardness, fuel range, cargo, hull, and scanner visibility. Future planet chapters can add further handling choices without making earlier worlds obsolete. Distinct hazards and traversal challenges should create choices, not turn the game into combat or add more survival meters. Core crossing, inverted gravity, radial drilling, core-powered travel, and uncapped progression are already part of the six-world campaign; future work should focus on playtesting, balance, and new chapters.
