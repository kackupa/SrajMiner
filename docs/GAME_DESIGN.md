# Game design — Cold Signal

## Campaign promise

Explore Vesper-9, an original ice moon holding the buried generation ship Faraday. Each expedition is a risk choice: take a modest haul home, or spend more fuel and cargo space to reach richer layers and recover survey leads. Ore sales improve the miner and fund a launch craft that opens new regions.

## Expedition loop

Launch from Hab 07, descend by drilling, and return through persistent tunnels. Deposits have deterministic yields of 0.5, 1, 2, or 3 units; their crystal density communicates value before cutting. Cargo has a strict shared capacity, and multi-tile cutters warn before a cut would exceed it. Sell ore, service the pod, and invest credits in upgrades or the long-range ship.

The Cryo Shelf contains four guaranteed luminous route-data seams along its central shaft. Each seam sits in a seed-independent, oval chamber that grows deeper into the campaign; chamber rims pulse softly, and its name appears once surveyed. Mining a seam records its route fragment permanently in the archive and pays a one-time Faraday salvage claim equal to the price of its matching ship component. All four claims cover the $2,800 ship cost, so the campaign route itself funds travel even when random ore rolls are poor. Four optional navigation hashes, one per region, add fictional offline checksum collectibles and short lore entries without paying credits or creating any exchangeable value. The archive also derives a survey ledger from each region's existing saved depth and collectible records, showing which regions have been visited and the deepest scan in each. Ore sales remain available for upgrades, services, and optional tools. The shipyard sells four components: frame, propulsion, navigation, and habitat core. Completing it unlocks free travel between Cryo Shelf, Hull Graveyard, Prism Fault, and Mars Frontier for migrated Mars saves. Every region has an independent seed and persistent excavation record. Each new visit begins at its own surface hub.

## Miner growth and risk

Seven upgrade tracks improve drill speed, fuel capacity, cargo capacity, hull integrity, engine thrust, scanner range, and automatic grapple reach. Tracks continue beyond level 5 with rising prices and diminishing stat gains; scanner levels after full horizontal coverage reveal deeper vertical bands. Drill levels 3–5 cut two, three, then four tiles in a centered swath, with later tiers gradually widening the cutter. Wide cuts use proportionally more drill fuel; the pod's compact collision body remains unchanged for shaft travel. The starter grapple catches a dangerous fall when clear, higher rock is within range; upgrades extend its reach and reduce its cooldown. It briefly hangs the pod so the player can thrust away, and never catches through solid rock. Fuel exhaustion or hull failure recovers the pod and forfeits unsold ore, while banked credits, upgrades, discoveries, and tunnels remain.

Only fuel, hull, and cargo need managing. Gravity itself uses no fuel. Long uncontrolled falls cause velocity-based hull damage; short drops are safe. Emergency recovery prevents a stranded or bankrupt save from becoming unplayable.

## World and presentation

Hab 07 develops into a vertical mining town as ship systems and planetary core records are completed. New one-way decks can be landed on and dropped through with Down, mirrored by local gravity on the far hemisphere. Elevator frames, work cabins, transit, archive antenna, and beacon make the permanent campaign milestones visible at the surface. Keep town growth tied to those existing milestones rather than adding recipe crafting or a new resource ledger.

Worlds are 48 tiles wide, streamed in deterministic 16×16 chunks, and effectively open-ended below. Seeded yields, ore distributions, cave frequency, palette, strata names, and per-region factors create different descents. Prism Fault adds rare luminous geodes: each is clearly outlined, called out by the drill target, and guarantees three units of ore. Mars Frontier has warm, orange thermal seams and Hull Graveyard has mint-lit ark-alloy caches; both are uncommon, give at least two units, and are highlighted in the target readout. All finds derive from map seed and tile coordinate, so they survive chunk reloads and old save formats need no change. The explored map shows dug tunnels only and never reveals hidden ore. Return-fuel telemetry estimates vertical ascent plus reserve; it cannot predict detours or piloting.

The art is original procedural pixel-style terrain and equipment. Mining charges purchased at Hab 07 open a small diamond-shaped blast after a readable 1.2-second fuse. They eject ore as physical pickups instead of placing it directly in cargo; cargo space and piloting determine how much the player recovers. The optional salvage magnet pulls loose pickups toward the pod within 190 pixels when no solid block obstructs the path; it still respects cargo capacity. Pickups, fractional remainder, and armed fuse state persist with the region. The scanner begins at four tiles and upgrades to the map's full 48-tile width. A purchasable stasis module holds vertical position while X is held, consuming fuel. From 240 m, a rock swimmer phases through intact geology, homes toward the miner, and glows within scanner range with an amber warning cue at close range; a collision deals 8 hull damage. Up to three automatic sentry turrets can intercept swimmers around built underground stations, adding a defensive build choice without direct combat controls or another meter. All seven upgrade tracks now contribute to miner artwork growth up to a 1.72× scale, while its collision body stays compact for excavated tunnels. An original quantized synth score gains rhythmic layers during movement and drilling; recovering route data or an archive hash adds a short, distinct four-note hook on the next beat. Quick mute and separate music/effects levels persist locally. At Hab 07, the long-range ship appears as an assembly-in-progress; installed components visibly fill in the craft, and a nearby status label changes from its system count to flight-ready. Pilot specializations offer faster deep-rock cutting, wider scanner reveal, or a larger ore hold; they are free to switch while docked, and the UI prevents leaving cargo above a smaller hold's limit. Visual ore cues keep resource value legible without audio. The Hab style bay sells pod finishes, pilot suit palettes, hull decals, and silhouette profiles; profiles pair a rendered frame shape with a cabin-glow color without changing collision or handling.

## Save and scope

The four optional regional navigation hashes unlock named crew voice logs in the archive. Recovering all four reveals the final message and closes the initial Faraday signal mystery; hash recovery reuses the existing milestone save list, so old saves need no migration. Each planet's sealed core sample independently reveals a short evidence record, while all four together unlock a route-home conclusion in the same archive. Core records also reuse their existing milestone IDs, so this story reveal needs no save migration.

Version 17 local saves keep campaign progression separate from each map's seed, location, excavation, exploration, ore pickups, armed charge, and built platforms, service beacon, defense turrets, escape-suit ownership, and an active pilot escape. Navigation-hash collection reuses the durable milestone list, so it requires no migration. Grapple cooldown and tether are momentary physics state and do not change save data. Version 1–16 saves migrate without changing their original excavation; older campaigns receive the balanced pilot path, starter scanner, grapple, and an unowned surface winch by default. Players can export JSON saves and import a validated save after reviewing its campaign summary. There is no backend, wallet, token, or blockchain integration. Navigation hashes are fictional, offline collectibles with no monetary value.

This is an expanding playable campaign foundation, not yet a finished long-form RPG. Further chapters beyond the four current regions, deeper crew/archive arcs, a richer restored-ship hub, and sustained human balance tests remain future work. The regional hashes now reveal the first named crew voice logs. See [the roadmap](NEXT_SPRINT.md) and [playtest findings](QA.md).

## Planet-core campaign direction

The long-term fantasy is to mine into the core of several distinct planets. Treat each destination as a planet with a recognizable sky, gravity/rock palette, strata, signature resources, hazards, and a route to a dangerous core chamber. The four current destinations now have different sealed core samples along the crossing path. Mining one records its planet-specific name and pays a one-time $600 archive salvage claim; the Archive tracks all four and marks the planetary ledger complete. This establishes a core objective on every current destination. The next progression step is to tie those records into authored story beats and a meaningful route unlock while keeping the familiar haul, sell, upgrade, and prepare loop between trips.

### Endgame: cross the core

The core is a midpoint that opens a second half of the same planet. After crossing the protected chamber, the far-side gravity points back toward the core; the 180-degree view reorientation makes this feel like an inverted hemisphere, and W thrusts outward toward its crust. Returning through the center flips the view and gravity back. The mine now has both surfaces, local depth and return-fuel readings, a one-time durable crossing reward, and a service dock at the far crust. A HUD cue explains the pull direction and outward thrust.

This uses 360-degree mouse drilling while steering or thrusting; upgraded swaths follow the aim vector and protect tiles overlapped by the pod. The flipped camera transform also reverses pointer aim and streamed tile rendering. Gravity-aware grapple, impact handling, physical drops, charges, hazards, local return estimate, docking, service, and save-derived orientation are implemented. System tests cover a far-crust return and mirrored aim/save state. A fresh-save browser expedition through crossing, drilling, return, reload, and crossing back is still required before tuning the experience or declaring the route verified. No crossing should strand a save or duplicate excavation.

### Endless-feeling upgrade curve

Upgrade tracks now continue beyond level 5 with rising prices and diminishing stat gains. The current purchase path is credit-based and available at the workshop; planet-tier unlocks and material requirements remain part of future planetary progression. Upgrade values, prices, pod scale, save validation, and workshop display support levels beyond 5. Old saves retain their exact current levels.

Future upgrades should answer a concrete planetary challenge: grapple stability, drill width/hardness, fuel range, cargo, hull, scanner visibility, and planet-specific handling. Add upgrade tiers alongside the planet curve so new depth bands ask for a better build without making earlier planets obsolete. Distinct hazards and traversal challenges should create choices, not turn the game into combat or add more survival meters. Core crossing, inverted gravity, radial drilling, and uncapped-by-five progression are endgame direction; the present campaign still uses its four current regional maps and one deep signal mystery.
