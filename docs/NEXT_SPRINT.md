# Campaign roadmap — Cold Signal

## Built and playable

- Original Vesper-9 / Faraday setting, with the original Mars presentation retained for migrated legacy saves.
- Persistent archive depth leads, an ore-credit-funded launch craft, and a destination board.
- Four deterministic regions with separate persistent tunnels and discovery state.
- Visible seeded ore yields (0.5, 1, 2, 3 units), fractional cargo, and proportional selling value.
- Drill-reactive, quantized synthesized music with saved mute and separate music/effects mix levels.
- Recovered route signals and archive hashes each add a distinct four-note hook on the next score beat, routed through the music mix.
- Explored-tunnel map that hides unseen ore, return-fuel estimate, and a conservative reserve indicator.
- Drill upgrades that cut 1/1/2/3/4 blocks by level and visibly enlarge pod artwork alongside cargo upgrades; the compact collision body still fits old tunnels.
- Four guaranteed route-data seams in authored, survey-labeled Cryo Shelf chambers, with durable archive records and a v4 save migration from older campaigns.
- Prism Fault's rare luminous geodes, which stand out visually and guarantee a three-unit ore payout.
- Mars Frontier's thermal seams and Hull Graveyard's ark-alloy caches; both are seed-stable, locally highlighted, and guarantee richer two-unit finds.
- Purchasable mining-charge packs, with a 1.2-second fuse, a 13-tile blast, and physical ore pickups that persist per region.
- Hab 07 paint bay with three credit-earned pod finishes, active paint preview, and save-backed ownership/equipment; paint is visual only.
- Style bay offers four pilot suit palettes, four hull decals, and four silhouette profiles alongside pod finishes; profiles also change cabin glow, and every cosmetic remains visual-only.
- Four guaranteed Cryo route fragments now pay one-time Faraday salvage claims matching the four ship-component costs ($2,800 total), so a completed chapter funds region travel without lucky ore rolls.
- Optional $420 salvage magnet reels physical charge drops toward the pod through open tunnels from 190 px; cargo capacity and solid walls still constrain recovery.
- Expedition briefing lists the movement/sale/map/pause controls and frames the cargo-versus-return-fuel decision before launch.
- Outpost archive records the unknown deep signal as an unresolved lead or recovered transmission with its paid bounty; existing save flags drive the display.
- The explored map marks nearby surveyed ore with a five-ore and signature-find legend; undiscovered blocks remain hidden.
- World crystals and explored-map markers use distinct ore-specific silhouettes, with the same shapes repeated in the map legend so color is not the only ore cue.
- The outpost archive keeps a per-region survey ledger: deepest scan, recovered Cryo route signals, and each region's optional hash; untouched regions remain clearly unvisited.
- The Pod Workshop lets the player choose a free Seam Cutter, Surveyor, Hauler, or Balanced specialization before a trip. Paths change hard-rock cutting speed, scanner reveal radius, or cargo capacity and can be switched while docked.
- Hab 07 shows the Faraday's assembly progress beside the visible component scaffold; the label changes to flight-ready when all four ship systems are installed.
- Each region contains one optional fictional navigation hash with a unique checksum and archive lore entry. Hashes are offline, have no cash value, and persist in the existing milestone list without a save migration.
- Each recovered regional hash unlocks a distinct named crew voice log. Four recordings connect the regional expeditions to the Faraday signal mystery; progress reuses existing hash milestones and adds no save fields.
- Recovering all four crew logs reveals Asha Vale's final message and triggers an archive-complete cue when the last hash is drilled; earlier records show a spoiler-free remaining count.
- Outpost Sell / Service / Upgrades tabs support one continuous visit, preserve the sale receipt, explain upgrade benefits/shortfalls, and keep keyboard focus inside the open dialog after transactions.
- Seeded dirt, basalt, and hard-rock surface details, connected surveyed ore veins, directional side-cut debris, and a distinct hard-rock impact cue add depth without changing saved geology.
- Full and partial cargo-loss warnings account for every ore tile in a multi-block cut and remain visible through the hold-to-cut delay; release cancels before terrain changes.
- The HUD warns at 168 px/s descent speed, before the 280 px/s landing-damage threshold, and tells the player to hold W to brake. Isolated held-key Chromium verified appearance at 197.5 px/s and dismissal at −27.5 px/s with full hull throughout. Player noticeability and effect on ordinary landings still need human judgment.

## Next priorities

- **Priority 1 — round-planet world and zooming camera; concept prototype browser-checked, game integration pending.** Treat every map as one connected round planet. Normal play should use a readable wrapping-map view; zooming out should smoothly bend it into a spherical cutaway, revealing the full surface loop, both settlements, the mined passage through the core, and the player's position. The [isolated camera sketch](prototypes/round-planet-camera.html) uses schematic tiles; a Chromium smoke check on 2026-10-08 verified the wrap-to-orbit slider, Mining View return, Full Planet shortcut, and a clean browser console. An isolated feature branch now has tested camera-focus interpolation, orbital fit scale, seam convergence, chart-to-screen projection, and v18 planet-chart save metadata with a rectangle-preserving v17 migration, but Phaser still renders the rectangular world. The [world geometry and implementation brief](PLANET_WORLD_DESIGN.md) documents the wrap seam, scale mismatch, migration path, and acceptance checks. Next, expand the seeded world and collision/movement model around the shared chart before building the globe renderer. Verify surface wrap, tunnels, gravity, aim, and markers at each zoom level. This remains the top new design task before expanding the other feel/presentation ideas.
- **Priority 2 — landing-gated safety grapple; human feel check and integration.** The grapple now predicts the pod's fall path and catches only when a damaging terrain impact is forecast within 1.5 seconds; safe catches and open-shaft falls do not trigger it. The isolated implementation passes 85 system tests and the production build. First, play a few falls with an available anchor: test a safe ledge, a long open shaft, and a fast approach to a hard floor. Confirm the hook fires in the last case, never in the first two, and still leaves time to brake. If the timing feels right, bring the isolated change into the playable checkout and verify the tether visually. Until then, the active game files remain unchanged.
- **Underground construction, defense, and escape — implemented; browser QA pending.** At 180–3,400 m, build gravity-aware one-way decks, a service beacon, or up to three auto-firing sentry turrets with carried ore and credits. Pack the $780 one-use escape suit at Hab 07 to eject after hull failure, jetpack through the mine, drop charges, and reach a surface dock or local beacon without drilling. Stops persist per map; beacons provide fuel and hull service, while ore sale and campaign progression still require returning to the surface. System tests cover affordability, valid sites, limits, gravity, and save migration.
- **Surface mining town — first playable pass implemented.** Hab 07 adds one-way sky decks that land correctly under both gravity orientations and can be dropped through with Down. Ship assembly and core records build the skyline in stages; the camera and service zone include the new decks. System tests cover stage unlocks, highest-deck landing, drop-through, and far-hemisphere gravity. A browser play session still needs to assess navigation, deck visibility on small viewports, and whether the town feels like a useful destination rather than a tall backdrop. Later expansion can add named NPCs or platform destinations after this traversal pass is player-tested.

- **Automatic recovery grapple — current playable behavior.** The checked-in game fires the starter auto-hook when the pod falls fast and a clear, higher anchor is within range. It suspends briefly, can be broken with W, and upgrade levels extend range/reduce cooldown. The landing-prediction change described in Priority 1 is isolated and is not yet part of the live checkout; compare the two behaviors in play before integration. Grapple levels persist in save v17 with v16 migration.
- **Core-story progression implemented; route and feel checks remain.** Every destination has a distinct sealed core sample, a one-time $600 claim, and an archive record that unlocks only when recovered. The four samples reveal a route-home story, with a final conclusion gated behind the complete ledger. This reuses existing milestones and requires no save migration. The next content step is a new planet chapter with its own geology, signature find, crew evidence, and core record; keep existing region travel freely available after ship assembly.
- **Endgame: cross through the core into inverted gravity.** The complete two-hemisphere traversal is implemented: gravity and view flip past the core; 360-degree mouse aim, upgraded radial drill swaths, grapple, fall handling, fuel estimates, physical drops, charges, hazards, docking, far-side service, and position persistence follow the new orientation. System tests simulate a far-side return and cover mirrored aim/save behavior. Still required before calling it shipped: fresh-save browser play through core crossing, mining, return/docking, reload, and a second crossing. Subjective pacing and far-side readability need human feedback.
- **Progression beyond level 5 — implemented, balance still open.** Every track now calculates continued level values and prices; fuel, hull, cargo, drill, engine, scanner, and grapple gains continue with diminishing returns. Scanner tiers past full horizontal coverage extend vertical survey depth. The workshop always offers the next level, and save validation accepts safe-integer levels above 5. System tests reach level 9 on every track and cover post-five saves. Human economy testing and progression unlocks tied to planet/core milestones remain open.
- **Miner visual progression and laser-reach roadmap — backlog.** The character/pod should visibly record the player's progress, so drill reach, pod growth, and equipment silhouette advance as one readable visual arc. Keep the starter tool limited to adjacent rock, then make reach unlock in distinct, milestone-priced steps alongside the existing drill-width progression. Suggested tiers: (1) **Field Bit** — compact stock drill, short contact sparks, and the adjacent-tile preview; (2) **Extended Auger** — a forward nose extension, rotating bit, stronger chip spray, and a one-tile reach cue; (3) **Resonance Lance** — a reinforced front assembly with a pulsing charge, expanding reticle, and two-step reach; (4) **Survey Bore** — larger articulated drill head, side braces that read clearly while turning, and a longer charge animation/beam preview; (5) **Laser Miner** — endgame emitter and heat fins, a stable colored beam with a bright contact flare, a brief overheat/cooldown animation, and the longest deliberate reach. Tier names and exact step count remain tuning choices. Art scope: layered procedural or sprite-based drill-head parts, tier-specific glow/impact particles and directional previews, silhouette variants that remain readable at the existing pod scale, and palette-safe beam/reticle colors for all planets. Preserve the compact collision body and distinguish reach from swath width in the workshop and HUD; balance both against fuel, cut time, and ore pickup distance. Use existing pod profile cosmetics as separate player choice, not a substitute for earned progression. If reach is added as durable state, preserve old saves at adjacent-only reach and add a save migration; test aim, 360-degree orientation, multi-cut clearance, and return trips at each tier.

### Design and feel backlog

- **Drilling animation variety.** Add several readable drill cycles so repeated mining feels responsive: steady cutting, hard-rock recoil, dust burst, occasional metal spark, breakthrough debris, and a brief valuable-ore glint. Vary effects by material (dust/crumbs for dirt, cracks/chunks for stone, sparks for metal, shards for crystal, frost for ice) and make the drill visibly extend toward the selected tile. Keep particles brief and inexpensive; use the reach-tier visual plan above so animation and tool length progress together.
- **Fall-aware camera and speed feedback.** Lead the camera downward in proportion to fall speed and hold the miner higher in frame, then ease smoothly back to the normal follow position after braking, landing, or climbing. At dangerous speeds, add restrained upward-rushing particles, a stronger wind cue, a small shake/zoom or stretch, and a braced fall pose. Keep the approaching floor and hazards sharp and readable; effects must communicate speed without masking the braking window.
- **Distinctive miner character design pass.** Review the current miner for a strong small-scale silhouette, clear front/drill direction, personality, and coherent upgrade stacking. Explore a compact industrial mini-mech direction with modular chassis, drill mount, movement base, fuel/power pack, winch attachment, and effect layers. Let major hull tiers change width, armor, tanks, lights, and movement hardware, while drill tiers change the tool; animate idle, movement, cutting, impacts, falls, braking, grapple catches, and winch pulls with matching weight. Produce a tier sheet and a reusable parts/animation asset list before replacing current art. Preserve movement clearance and distinguish earned progression from optional cosmetic profiles.
- **Winch as a visible extraction system.** Expand the existing Surface Winch fantasy with a cable that remains attached and spools visibly as the miner descends, then tightens and hauls the miner back through the open route when activated. Show estimated fuel cost before committing; include cable slack/tension, motor and arrival cues, a short tension jerk, and a retrieval pose. Keep the pull grounded in cleared passages, account for depth and cargo in fuel/speed tuning, and avoid clipping or stranding the miner when a shaft is blocked. Treat this as an extension of the existing winch upgrade, not a second return mechanic.
- **Core transition direction: player-test before redesign.** The pasted design note questions mirrored controls and suggests using the core as an endpoint or biome transition with familiar controls. This conflicts with the requested inverted-gravity second hemisphere already in the game, so do not remove it by assumption. Playtest crossing, orientation, and return readability first; compare a controlled cinematic transition with unchanged controls against the current inversion and use player feedback to decide whether the core remains a world flip or becomes a new biome/goal transition.
Combat, extra survival meters, recipe crafting, monetization, and online systems remain outside the game scope.
